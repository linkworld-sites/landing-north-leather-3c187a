If you've compared a leather belt against a synthetic one and wondered which actually lasts, the honest answer depends on what "leather" means on the label — because a lot of what's sold as leather is built the same way as the synthetic version, just with a thinner coating of the real thing on top.

## What a synthetic belt is made of

A synthetic or "bonded" belt is a backing material — often a fiber or scrap composite — with a printed or coated surface layer bonded on top to look like leather. It's built in layers because the backing alone isn't presentable, and the surface layer alone isn't strong enough. That construction can look convincing on a shelf. It's the folding, not the shelf, that tells you what it's made of.

## Where each one actually fails

A synthetic belt's coating is the first thing to go. It cracks at the fold — the same crease, worn in at the same spot every day — and once it cracks, it peels, leaving the backing exposed and unraveling underneath. The buckle is usually plated rather than solid, so the finish wears through at the edges where it rubs against clothing, showing a duller metal underneath.

The hide creases and softens with wear instead of splitting where layers meet, and the raw brass tarnishes and darkens rather than wearing down to a duller metal.

## Why the direction of aging is the real difference

This is the part a spec sheet won't show you: a synthetic belt gets worse with use, because use is what cracks the coating and wears the plating. A full-grain leather belt gets better with use — it darkens, it creases where it flexes, it takes on a patina that a coated surface can't develop because there's nothing underneath it to age. One is manufactured to look right at the start and decline from there. The other is built to look better a year in than it did on day one.

## A quick way to check before you buy

- Fold it hard, at the buckle end, and look at the crease. A coating will show a faint white line or crack starting. Full-grain hide just creases.
- Ask what's under the surface. If the answer involves a "backing" or "lining," that's the layer doing the real work, and the layer that fails first.
- Check whether the hardware is plated or raw. Plating wears through; raw brass just tarnishes.
- Expect the belt to change over time. If it's not supposed to change at all, it's probably not leather all the way through.
