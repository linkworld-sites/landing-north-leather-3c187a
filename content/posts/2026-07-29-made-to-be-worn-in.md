We didn't start North Leather to make more bags. We started it because most bags are built to be replaced.

Full-grain leather, cut once and stitched by hand, doesn't work that way. It scuffs before it softens. It darkens where your hand rests, and lightens where the sun finds it. Ten years in, it looks better than it did on day one — not despite the wear, because of it.

That's the whole idea. Not a slogan, a material fact.

## What "full-grain" actually means

Most leather sold today is corrected: sanded down to hide flaws, then printed with a fake grain pattern to look consistent. It looks fine in a showroom and stays exactly the same for the rest of its life, because there's nothing left in it to change.

Full-grain is the opposite. It's the top layer of the hide, uncorrected — every scar, wrinkle, and pore left in place. It's harder to work with. It's also the only layer that ages the way people actually want leather to age: developing a patina, not falling apart.

We vegetable-tan it, the slow way — weeks in tannin baths instead of a few hours in chromium salts. It costs more. It's also the only tanning process the leather trade has trusted for centuries, for exactly this reason.

## Built by hand, on purpose

Every panel is cut, skived, and saddle-stitched by hand in one workshop — two needles, one waxed thread, pulled through pre-punched holes so the seam gets *stronger* under tension, not weaker. Raw brass hardware, left unlacquered, so it ages alongside the leather instead of flaking off it.

None of this is the fast way to build a bag. It's the only way to build one that's still working in ten years.

## Buy it once

We're not chasing seasons, drops, or trend cycles. We make full-grain, vegetable-tanned bags, built to be used until they're passed down rather than thrown out. If that sounds like the opposite of everything else being sold to you right now — it is, on purpose.
