Buying a full-grain leather bag instead of a fast-fashion one is the first decision. The second is smaller and matters almost as much: how you look after it in the years between. Full-grain leather doesn't need much — that's part of the appeal — but the little it does need makes the difference between a bag that ages well and one that just gets old.

## Let it get wet, then let it dry slowly

Full-grain leather can take rain. What it can't take is a radiator, a heater vent, or direct sun while it's still damp — fast, forced drying pulls the oils out of the hide and leaves it stiff and prone to cracking. If it gets caught in weather, wipe off standing water, stuff it loosely with a towel to hold its shape, and let it dry at room temperature, away from direct heat. It'll be fine by morning.

## Condition it twice a year, not twice a month

A light leather conditioner — nothing with silicone or heavy waxes — keeps the hide supple as it darkens. Twice a year is enough for a bag in regular use; more than that just leaves it oversaturated and soft in the wrong way. Work a small amount in with a soft cloth, let it sit for a few minutes, then buff off the excess. You're feeding the leather, not coating it.

## Let the marks stay

This is the one piece of advice that surprises people: don't try to erase every scuff. A scratch on corrected, coated leather is permanent damage — it's cut through the printed finish to bare backing. A scratch on full-grain is usually gone within a week, buffed out by the natural oils in the hide and the friction of ordinary use. The scuffs, the darkening at the corners, the crease where the strap sits on your shoulder — that's the patina doing its job. It's evidence the bag is being lived with, not a flaw to correct.

## Keep the brass honest

Raw brass hardware will tarnish. Leave it alone and it settles into a deep, even patina that matches the leather's own aging. If you prefer it brighter, a small amount of brass polish on a soft cloth brings back the shine — but there's no wrong answer here; unlacquered brass is designed to be worn either way.

## Store it upright, and use it

A leather bag kept in a dust bag on a shelf for months at a time creases and stiffens in whatever position it's left in. The best storage for a full-grain bag is regular use — the oils and fibers stay flexible under normal handling in a way they don't under long storage. If it does need to sit for a stretch, keep it upright, lightly stuffed, out of direct light.

None of this is complicated, and none of it is optional in the way that, say, dry-cleaning instructions for a fast-fashion garment are optional because the garment won't survive long enough for it to matter. A little attention here is the whole reason a bag like this is still working in ten years instead of three.
